Crispy Oven-Baked Cod Sandwiches with Dill-Pickle Slaw & Potato Wedges

Crunchy panko-coated white fish on whole-wheat buns with a bright yogurt-pickle slaw and crisp potato wedges.

Ingredients

Potato wedges

  • 1½ pounds (680 g) Yukon Gold or russet potatoes, cut into wedges
  • 2 teaspoons olive oil
  • ½ teaspoon smoked paprika
  • ½ teaspoon garlic powder
  • ½ teaspoon kosher salt

Fish

  • 1½ pounds (680 g) cod or haddock fillets, cut into 4 portions and patted dry
  • ¼ cup (30 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1 large egg, beaten with 1 tablespoon water
  • 1 cup (60 g) panko breadcrumbs
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • ½ teaspoon garlic powder
  • ½ teaspoon kosher salt
  • Black pepper and olive-oil spray

Dill-pickle slaw and assembly

  • 10 ounces (283 g) plain coleslaw mix
  • ½ cup (120 g) plain nonfat Greek yogurt
  • ⅓ cup finely chopped dill pickles
  • 1 tablespoon pickle brine
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh dill
  • 4 whole-wheat hamburger buns
  • Lemon wedges

Tip
Dry the fish well before breading. Press the panko firmly onto each portion and spray the coating lightly for better browning.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 450°F. Toss the potato wedges with olive oil, paprika, garlic powder and salt. Roast on a preheated sheet pan for 25–35 minutes, turning once.
  2. Set a wire rack on a second rimmed sheet pan and coat it with cooking spray. Season the flour, egg wash and panko separately; add the paprika, garlic powder, salt and pepper to the panko.
  3. Dredge each fish portion in flour, then egg, then panko. Press the crumbs on firmly, place on the rack and mist the tops with olive-oil spray.
  4. Bake for 10–14 minutes, until the coating is crisp and the fish flakes easily and reaches 145°F.
  5. Stir the yogurt, pickles, brine, lemon juice and dill together. Fold in the slaw mix just before serving.
  6. Toast the buns if desired. Build each sandwich with fish and slaw; serve with potato wedges and lemon.

Plan note
Cod and haddock both work. Choose whichever looks fresher and costs less.

Sweet & Fiery Pork Tenderloin with Mango Salsa, Cilantro-Lime Rice & Broccoli

Mild sweet-chili-glazed pork tenderloin with fresh mango salsa, jasmine rice and roasted broccoli. No hot honey or mustard.

Electric oven + stovetop · About 55 minutes · 4 servings. Meat weights are raw unless stated otherwise; dry rice/pasta quantities are uncooked.

Weekly menu · Obsidian note · Shopping list

Ingredients

  • 1¾ lb pork tenderloin, trimmed (one large or two small pieces)
  • 1 large ripe mango, peeled and diced
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced (salsa)
  • 2 tbsp lime juice for salsa, plus 1 tbsp for rice
  • ¼ cup chopped fresh cilantro, divided
  • 3 tbsp Thai sweet red chili sauce (not hot honey)
  • ½ tsp Sriracha, optional, for mild heat
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • ½ tsp ground ginger
  • ¾ cup dry jasmine rice
  • 1¼ cups water for rice, or use rice package directions
  • 1½ lb broccoli florets
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil, divided
  • ½ tsp kosher salt plus to taste
  • ¼ tsp ground black pepper

Method

  1. Heat oven to 400°F. Pat pork dry; rub with 1 tsp olive oil, garlic powder, ginger, salt and pepper. Roast on a rack over a sheet pan until approaching 125–130°F internally, about 18–25 minutes depending on thickness.
  2. While pork roasts, mix chopped mango, minced garlic, 2 tbsp lime juice, 2 tbsp cilantro and a pinch of salt. Refrigerate until serving.
  3. Mix Thai sweet chili sauce with optional Sriracha; skip the Sriracha for milder flavor. Brush the pork with glaze and return to the oven about 5–10 minutes, until the center reaches 145°F. Rest at least 3 minutes, preferably 8, before slicing.
  4. Cook jasmine rice in water according to package directions. Fold in remaining cilantro and 1 tbsp lime juice. Roast broccoli on a second sheet pan at 425°F with remaining olive oil and a little salt for 18–22 minutes.
  5. Divide sliced pork into four portions; top with mango salsa. Serve with cilantro-lime rice and broccoli.

Avoid adding honey or mustard: the mild sweet-chili sauce provides the glaze. Check pork with a thermometer; do not rely on a fixed roast time.

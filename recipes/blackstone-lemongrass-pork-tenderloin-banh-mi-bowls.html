Blackstone Lemongrass Pork Tenderloin Banh Mi Bowls

Caramelized lemongrass pork tenderloin over rice with quick-pickled vegetables, cucumber, herbs and a light spicy yogurt drizzle.

Ingredients

Pork

  • 1½ pounds (680 g) pork tenderloin, trimmed and sliced ¼ inch thick across the grain
  • 2 tablespoons minced lemongrass or refrigerated lemongrass paste
  • 2 tablespoons low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon fish sauce
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar or honey
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • Black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon neutral high-heat oil

Bowls

  • 1 cup (185 g) dry jasmine rice, cooked
  • 2 medium carrots, julienned or shredded
  • 1 English cucumber, thinly sliced
  • ½ cup rice vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • ½ teaspoon kosher salt
  • ½ cup cilantro leaves
  • ¼ cup sliced scallions
  • 1 jalapeño, thinly sliced, optional

Light drizzle

  • ½ cup (120 g) plain nonfat Greek yogurt
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice
  • 1–2 teaspoons sriracha
  • Water and salt, as needed

Tip
Freeze the tenderloin for 20 minutes before slicing. The firmer meat is much easier to cut thinly.

Method

  1. Combine the pork with lemongrass, soy sauce, fish sauce, brown sugar, garlic, sesame oil and black pepper. Marinate for 20 minutes or refrigerate up to 8 hours.
  2. Cook the rice. Stir the carrots with the rice vinegar, sugar and salt; let stand at least 20 minutes, then drain.
  3. Whisk the yogurt, lime juice and sriracha with enough water to make a spoonable drizzle.
  4. Heat the Blackstone to medium-high. Add the neutral oil, spread the pork in a single layer and cook for 2–3 minutes per side until browned and at least 145°F. Work in batches if needed. A large skillet works the same way indoors.
  5. Divide rice among four bowls. Add pork, quick-pickled carrots, cucumber, cilantro, scallions and jalapeño. Finish with the yogurt drizzle.

Plan note
The tenderloin keeps the banh mi flavors while giving the bowl a leaner protein base.

Nutrition

Approximately ~500 calories and ~45 g protein per serving. Nutrition varies with brands and serving choices.

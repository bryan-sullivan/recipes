Thai-Marinated Steak Salad

Fish sauce, lime, ginger and chile give this salad its sharp, savory backbone. The dressing is reserved before the raw steak touches the marinade.

Ingredients

Steak and marinade

  • 1½ pounds (680 g) flank steak, trimmed
  • 3 tablespoons fresh lime juice
  • 2 tablespoons low-sodium soy sauce
  • 1½ tablespoons fish sauce
  • 1 tablespoon light brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon grated fresh ginger
  • 2 garlic cloves, grated
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon sambal oelek or sriracha

Salad

  • 8 cups chopped romaine
  • 1 large English cucumber, halved and sliced
  • 1 pint cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1 cup shredded carrots
  • ½ small red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 cup fresh mint leaves
  • 1 cup fresh cilantro leaves
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges

Light peanut dressing

  • ¼ cup (26 g) powdered peanut butter
  • 2 tablespoons (30 ml) fresh lime juice, for dressing
  • 1 teaspoon (7 g) honey
  • 2–4 tablespoons water, as needed

Also use the ¼ cup clean marinade reserved below.

Method

  1. Whisk lime juice, soy sauce, fish sauce, brown sugar, ginger, garlic, sesame oil and sambal. Reserve ¼ cup in a clean container before adding raw steak. Whisk this clean portion with the powdered peanut butter, dressing lime juice, honey and enough water for a pourable dressing.
  2. Marinate the flank steak in the remaining mixture for 1–4 hours.
  3. Heat the Blackstone or a heavy stovetop skillet over medium-high heat, or set the Kamado for two-zone cooking at 450°F. Pat steak dry and cook 4–6 minutes per side, adjusting heat as needed. Use temperature, not time alone, to determine doneness.
  4. For medium-rare, pull at 130–135°F. For USDA guidance, cook whole beef steak to 145°F and rest at least 3 minutes.
  5. Rest the steak 10 minutes, then slice thinly across the grain.
  6. Toss romaine, cucumber, tomatoes, carrots, onion, mint and cilantro with the light peanut dressing. Top with sliced steak and lime wedges.

Nutrition estimate
Approximately 430 calories and 43 g protein per serving, based on four servings. Brand choice and portion size will change the total.

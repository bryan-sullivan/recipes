Blackened White Fish with Key-Lime Tartar

A deliberately bold blackening blend gives mild white fish a smoky, peppery crust. Choose mahi-mahi, grouper or red snapper; all pair well with the cold Key-lime tartar.

Mustard-free
The tartar sauce contains mayonnaise, yogurt, Key lime, pickles, capers and herbs—no mustard or mustard powder.

Ingredients

Fish and blackening

  • 1½ pounds mahi-mahi, grouper or red snapper fillets, cut into 4 portions
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 2 teaspoons sweet paprika
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • ½ teaspoon ground cumin
  • ¼–½ teaspoon cayenne
  • ¾ teaspoon kosher salt
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons neutral high-heat oil

Mustard-free Key-lime tartar

  • ½ cup light mayonnaise
  • ½ cup plain nonfat Greek yogurt
  • 2 Key limes or 1 Persian lime, zested and juiced
  • 2 tablespoons finely chopped dill pickles
  • 1 tablespoon capers, rinsed and chopped
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh cilantro or parsley

Sides

  • 2 cups cooked wild-and-brown rice blend
  • 4 cups steamed green beans or asparagus

Method

  1. Mix mayonnaise, yogurt, lime zest, 1 tablespoon lime juice, pickles, capers and herbs. This tartar is explicitly mustard-free. Refrigerate until serving.
  2. Combine both paprikas, thyme, oregano, garlic powder, onion powder, cumin, cayenne, salt and pepper. Pat the fish very dry and press the seasoning firmly onto all sides.
  3. Set the Kamado Joe for direct cooking at 450°F with a cast-iron plancha or skillet preheating for at least 10 minutes.
  4. Lightly oil the hot surface. Cook the fish without moving it for 2–4 minutes, depending on thickness, until a dark crust forms.
  5. Flip once and cook 2–4 minutes more. Pull the fish when it flakes at the edges and reaches 145°F in the center.
  6. Serve immediately with the cold Key-lime tartar, wild-and-brown rice and green vegetables.

Approximately 370 calories and 36.5 g protein per serving, based on four servings. Brand choice and portion size will change the total.
